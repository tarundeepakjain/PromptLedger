## Interview 1 — Avadhesh Nagar

**Role:** Student  
**Organization/Stage:** IIIT Vadodara Student 
**Interview Duration:** ~15 minutes  

I spoke with Avadhesh Nagar to understand whether PromptLedger would also be useful for students and small builders experimenting with AI tools. The conversation helped validate that the strongest audience is not students, but early-stage startups and small teams actively paying for AI subscriptions.

### Direct Quotes

> “Your product is good mainly targeting initial startups.”

> “Students can use it, but students mostly prefer free tools and don’t have that much workload.”

> “Why are you asking monthly price for subscriptions if the prices are already fixed?”

> “Can you take API input as monthly price instead of input and output tokens?”

> “The frontend is good and the UI is clean and easy to understand.”

---

## Most Surprising Insight

The most surprising part of the conversation was realizing that students are not a strong target audience even if they heavily use AI tools.

Initially, I assumed students and early builders would also benefit significantly from AI spend optimization. However, Avadhesh pointed out that most students:
- use free plans,
- switch tools frequently,
- and usually do not spend enough monthly money for optimization to matter.

This shifted my thinking toward founders, startups, agencies, and small teams where AI costs directly affect operational spending.

---

## Feedback About Pricing Inputs

Avadhesh questioned why the product asks users to manually enter monthly spend for subscriptions when many plans already have publicly known pricing.

This helped me clarify an important product decision:
- some enterprise contracts,
- regional pricing,
- annual billing discounts,
- bundled credits,
- and negotiated team plans

can create real-world spending differences from official pricing pages.

For API tools, he suggested allowing users to enter direct monthly spending instead of requiring token-based calculations because most non-technical founders do not understand token pricing clearly.

This was valuable usability feedback and highlighted that simplifying API cost input could improve onboarding significantly.

---

## What Changed In My Design Thinking

This interview changed two major assumptions:

### 1. Students are not the primary customer
I refined the GTM focus toward:
- startup founders,
- small SaaS teams,
- agency operators,
- and AI-heavy startups.

These users have actual recurring AI operational costs and stronger incentives to optimize spending.

### 2. Simpler API spend input may improve UX
Instead of requiring users to think in tokens, future versions could support:
- “estimated monthly API spend”
- or hybrid calculations with optional advanced settings.

This would reduce friction for non-technical users while still allowing accurate audits.

Overall, the conversation validated that the product direction is strongest when positioned as a startup AI spend optimization platform rather than a general AI tool tracker.
